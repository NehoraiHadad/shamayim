Reviewed `fcd96b26` read-only. The real-style rule shortens paths beyond a town-wide cap, including paths to some neighbouring buildings. It does not give all town walls the same veil, and the drawn style bypasses it.

## Findings

1. **High — Solar altitude changes the extinction path, even in fog and dust.** `src/scene/model/wallDistance.ts:36–38`, `buildingExposure.ts:19`, `landVeil.ts:34–66`, `src/world/air.ts:99`. At fixed air conditions, solar altitude changes the illumination and colour of scattered light. Extinction still depends on the path and the intervening air. Here twilight shortens that path regardless of weather. At full blend with a 1 km cap, a 2 km wall in fog with 2 km visibility and `fog=1` loses veil from **0.9604 to 0.7372**. With dust reducing visibility to 6 km and `fog=0`, it loses **0.4671 to 0.2019**. The fog multiplier and dust extinction still apply, but both operate on the shortened path. **Fix:** keep extinction at the wall’s depth and use the existing facade exposure for twilight contrast. If an artistic common veil remains required, blend it separately and fade its weight continuously to zero in fog and dust. **Test:** sweep altitude, fog and dust at fixed geometry and assert distance ordering, continuity and unchanged extinction in the physical path.

2. **High — The cap applies to the wrong receivers and fails to unify the right ones.** `outsideShape.ts:76–81,93–106,120–129`, `outside.ts:53–62,82`, `outsideMarks.ts:18–33,48–52`, `landmarkAtmosphere.ts:26–32`, `farCities.ts:29–38`. With a 1 km cap and 25 km visibility, own walls at 0.5 and 2 km get **0.0050 and 0.0185** at full blend, because `min` leaves the closer wall alone. A near-neighbour block at a 5 km tier drops from **0.2592 to 0.0185**, while a famous non-own glyph at the same 5 km keeps the true-distance input and a far-city tier at 5 km keeps its own veil. Seated marks can lose the support block’s distance through the same blanket transform. In the own-land branch, the day/fog path is already capped before rounding: 2 km over 1 km land becomes 1 km at every altitude. Even a 1.08 km cap can round up to 1.0905 km. The receiver path also feeds roofs, glass and roof accessories. **Fix:** carry ownership and depth separately, preserve own depth for the physical path, and apply any shared artistic veil only to the intended town’s walls and marks. **Test:** put near and deep own walls, an own glyph, a neighbour block and seated glyph, a famous non-own glyph and a far-city tier in one fixture. Assert each distance in day, fog and twilight.

3. **Medium — The fallback is an object-count mean of raw foot samples, and the missing-data arithmetic can produce `NaN`.** `wallDistance.ts:8–25,38`, `outsideShape.ts:111–129,148`, `src/world/outside.ts:580–638`, `outsideGround.ts:119–126`, `planeLight.ts:22–29`. With no own polygon, feet at `[1,1,4]` produce **2 km**. If two descriptors represent the same ground cell, averaging distinct cells gives **2.5 km**. Clearance-rejected buildings still vote. The samples are raw projected depths, while the plane painter uses a grid and smooths veil across it. With an own polygon, the scalar is the centre-sight distance the polygon actually uses for painting. One finite foot becomes the entire cap, even for a one-row town whose wall depth differs from the ground sample. No finite feet returns `Infinity`, which is benign for finite wall paths. An infinite wall path produces `NaN` even with blend weight zero, and a `NaN` polygon distance poisons every path. **Fix:** if retaining the visual rule, define the fallback over distinct painted ground cells with explicit area weights, validate distances, and return the original path when the target is missing or the weight is zero. **Test:** duplicate a descriptor, suppress it with clearance, and exercise one cell, no cells, invalid land and an infinite path.

4. **Medium — Drawn buildings and glyphs skip the rule.** `drawnOutsideLight.ts:23–34`, `outside.ts:50–56,89`, `src/scene/paint/drawn/town.ts:31`. For 2 km against a 1 km cap at full blend and 25 km visibility, drawn receives its original 2 km tier, whose illustrated veil is **0.0635**. The real receiver gets **0.0185**. **Fix:** if retaining a visual veil, resolve it once by ownership before branching into the style-specific colour calculations, and key the resulting drawn form and glyph tones. **Test:** compare both style descriptors across the same altitude sweep, holding geometry and other lighting inputs fixed.

5. **Medium — The real light key can reuse a different receiver byte and can churn during a scrub.** `outside.ts:85–89`, `receiverKey.ts:7–16`, `buildingLight.ts:51–57,111–119`, `src/scene/compose/paintJob.ts:27–39`. At 25 km visibility, effective paths of 1 and 1.015 km both encode veil key **5**, yet a black surface against unit airlight encodes **37 and 38** in sRGB. That is an isolated receiver-input collision with other key fields held fixed. Conversely, seven synthetic tiers spanning 2–10 km against a 1 km cap change the veil key on **56 of 60** successive 0.1° samples on the night ramp. This measures scrub sensitivity, not live frame frequency. Both paint paths consume the same precomputed descriptors and light keys. **Fix:** make paint and key use the same inputs at a precision verified in display space, or key the receiver bytes. **Test:** compare cached and fresh pixels on both paint paths at a byte boundary and count light rerasterizations across a scrub. The ground sampling is on shape-cache misses. Per-world receiver caches avoid repeated work at identical distances, but partial blend retains distinct distance entries. No render timing was measured.

6. **Medium — Existing tests can miss removal of the rule.** `buildingTwilight.test.ts:50–81` calls `buildingFaces(world, 0, id)`. `outside.test.ts:539–576` checks legacy tier tones or direct `tonesAt` calls. `src/scene/paint/outside.test.ts:288–323` bounds changes without requiring any twilight convergence. At −12.1° versus −11.9°, even the synthetic 10 km wall changes veil by only **0.00026** with the rule and **0** without it. **Fix/test:** add an `outsideLayer` assertion for the actual receiver veil, with a positive contrast between day and full blend if the effect is retained. Check that removing the rule fails that assertion. Keep the continuity tests as separate coverage.
